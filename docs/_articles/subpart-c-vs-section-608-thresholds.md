## Evaluate both frameworks independently

[40 CFR 82.157](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-40/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-82/subpart-F/section-82.157) generally covers qualifying appliances with at least 50 pounds of refrigerant containing an ozone-depleting substance, including blends. From January 1, 2026, [40 CFR 84.106](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-40/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-84/subpart-C/section-84.106) covers qualifying 15+ pound appliances containing a regulated substance, or a substitute for a regulated substance with GWP greater than 53. That GWP cutoff belongs to the substitute branch. Refrigerant identity alone does not determine the answer.

**Solely ODS appliances and residential/light-commercial air-conditioning and heat pumps are excluded from Part 84's leak-repair requirements.** This does not remove separate certification, recovery or venting duties. Confirm the appliance's regulatory subsector and refrigerant composition instead of relying on a generic AC label. ODS-containing blends can require review under both frameworks.

| Covered category | Part 82 | Part 84 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Comfort cooling | **10%** | **10%** |
| Commercial refrigeration | **20%** | **20%** |
| Industrial process refrigeration | **30%** | **30%** |
| Refrigerated transport, when covered | **10%** | **10%** |

The earlier comparison of 15% versus 10% on this site was wrong. Crossing means **exceeding** the threshold, not merely equaling it. Part 84 also assigns 10% to covered other appliances, including transport categories under the current rule; a proposed exclusion is not an enacted change.

Part 84 has a special first-addition rule beginning in 2026, subject to its stated exceptions. Verification timing, recordkeeping and inspections must be evaluated against the relevant paragraphs. This short comparison is not a complete compliance checklist. Keep the original evidence and document corrections instead of silently rewriting historical determinations.
